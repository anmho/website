Do not surround yourself with pessimism. Use skepticism to find the risks, then build a positive thesis and act on it. A business, a bet, or a life direction needs a case for why it can work; do not let someone else's resignation become your ceiling. Choose people who tell you the truth without making failure your identity.

This is a daily reminder, not a ban on skepticism or honest disagreement. [Experimental research on emotional contagion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4066473/) found that the emotional expressions people encountered influenced the emotional language they later expressed, although the measured effects were small and the study does not prove that every relationship works this way. The [American Psychological Association's overview of self-fulfilling prophecy](https://dictionary.apa.org/self-fulfilling-prophecy) describes how expectations can help bring about their own fulfillment.

Michael Burry is a useful example of the distinction. His famous housing short was bearish about mortgage bonds, but it was not passive pessimism; it was an active, researched thesis and a conviction that the bet would work. His [account of the trade](https://www.michael-burry.com/michael-burry/) and [later explanation of the investment opportunity](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/13/michael-burry-transcript/) support the reminder: skepticism can reveal the opportunity, but conviction is what lets you act on it.
