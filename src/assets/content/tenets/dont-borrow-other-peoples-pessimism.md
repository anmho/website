Do not surround yourself with pessimism. Seek criticism that helps you see risk clearly, but do not make resignation your environment. Choose people who tell you the truth without making failure your identity. Use pessimism as a tool for preparation, not a reason to avoid every possibility.

This is a daily reminder, not a ban on skepticism or honest disagreement. [Experimental research on emotional contagion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4066473/) found that the emotional expressions people encountered influenced the emotional language they later expressed, although the measured effects were small and the study does not prove that every relationship works this way. The [American Psychological Association's overview of self-fulfilling prophecy](https://dictionary.apa.org/self-fulfilling-prophecy) describes how expectations can help bring about their own fulfillment. Together, these ideas suggest a useful boundary: keep people who improve your judgment close, and be cautious around people whose default expectation is that nothing can work.
