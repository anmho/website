Treat common wisdom as a starting point, not a verdict. Ask what a claim assumes, when it holds, and how your circumstances change the answer. Seek nuance that improves judgment, not an excuse to avoid deciding.

“Startups are risky”: which risk, for whom, and what does the evidence about this team and business change? Specific evidence should update your judgment, not manufacture certainty.

“You can't get rich on a salary”: what income, savings, time horizon, and definition of rich? Earning in an expensive city does not obligate you to spend your life there. Consider where you want your savings to support you.

Source: A personal qualification of [Naval's argument about renting out your time](https://nav.al/renting-time). [BEA regional price parities](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area) document differences in purchasing power across US locations, not a guaranteed path to wealth.
